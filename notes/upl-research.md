# Unauthorized practice of law — research findings

Research memo, September 9, 2026. **Nothing here has been independently verified by a member
of the team.** Under the syllabus each of us is personally responsible for every claim we
publish, so read the sources before relying on any of this. Citations are given so that is
possible.

---

## 1. The line, as best it can be stated

**The statutes.** [§ 484.010.1](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=484.010)
defines "practice of law" as advocacy or "the drawing of papers, pleadings or documents … in
connection with proceedings pending or prospective before any court of record." It is tied to
proceedings and has no fee element. § 484.010.2 defines "law business" as "the advising or
counseling **for a valuable consideration** … as to any secular law," or "the drawing or the
procuring of or assisting in the drawing **for a valuable consideration** of any paper,
document or instrument affecting or relating to secular rights."

**Every prong of the law-business definition requires valuable consideration.**
[§ 484.020](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=484.020) makes violation a
misdemeanor (≤$100 fine) and creates a private action for **treble the amount paid**, plus an
action by the attorney general.

**The judicial gloss controls.** The statutes "merely act in aid of" the Missouri Supreme
Court's power and "cannot supersede or detract from" it; the judiciary is "sole arbiter of
what constitutes the practice of law." *[Hargis v. JLB
Corp.](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5281434/hargis-v-jlb-corp/)*, 357 S.W.3d 574,
578 (Mo. banc 2011).

The operative Missouri rules, in order of usefulness:

- **Selling self-help materials is permitted.** Sale of blank forms plus general instructions
  is not UPL "so long as [sellers] refrain from giving **personal advice as to legal remedies
  or the consequences flowing therefrom**." *[In re
  Thompson](https://law.justia.com/cases/missouri/supreme-court/1978/60074-0.html)*, 574
  S.W.2d 365, 369 (Mo. banc 1978).
- **Charging a fee to complete legal documents is close to per se UPL.** "[T]he act of
  charging a separate fee for the completion of legal forms by non-lawyers constitutes the
  unauthorized practice of law business." *Carpenter v. Countrywide*, 250 S.W.3d 697, 702
  (Mo. banc 2008). *Hargis* states it affirmatively: filling in standardized forms is not UPL
  "so long as no fee is charged and legal judgment or discretion is not involved."
- **Gathering information is not itself UPL.** *In re Mid-America*, 927 S.W.2d 855, 865.
- **Telling a user their situation complies with the law is UPL.** *[Lucas Subway MidMo v.
  Mandatory Poster
  Agency](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/5448673/lucas-subway-midmo-inc-v-mandatory-poster-agency-inc/)*,
  524 S.W.3d 116 (Mo. App. W.D. 2017) — representing that documents would put the customer
  "in full compliance" was, "[a]lthough rudimentary," the exercise of legal judgment.

**Is Missouri's reputation for aggressiveness earned?** Partly, and the mechanism matters.
What is aggressive is the **private treble-damages bar**, which has produced a sustained line
of class actions. Regulator enforcement looks quieter — in *Lucas Subway* the Office of Chief
Disciplinary Counsel investigated and declined to act. The practical risk is a plaintiff-side
suit, and its remedy is a multiple of **fees paid**.

## 2. What *Janson* actually establishes

*[Janson v. LegalZoom.com](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2173333/janson-v-legalzoom-com-inc/)*,
802 F. Supp. 2d 1053 (W.D. Mo. Aug. 2, 2011) (Laughrey, J.).

**Posture.** The court **granted** LegalZoom summary judgment on patent/trademark claims and
**denied it in all other respects**. It granted plaintiffs partial summary judgment on one
narrow element. It did **not** hold LegalZoom liable — the question was whether "a reasonable
juror could conclude" UPL occurred. The parties settled ten days later; final approval and
dismissal with prejudice followed April 30, 2012. No trial, no merits judgment, no appellate
review. It is one district judge's *Erie* prediction — persuasive only.

**Anyone who writes "the court held LegalZoom engaged in UPL" has overstated it.**

**What it does say is favorable.** The blank-forms offering was **not** UPL, and the court
volunteered: "Nor would LegalZoom be engaging in the unauthorized practice of law if it sold
general instructions to accompany those blank forms over the internet." And: "That
Defendant's legal document service is delivered through the internet is not the problem. The
internet is merely a medium."

**The distinction is narrower than commonly reported.** It was not "blank forms vs.
answer-driven software." Two things drove it: (1) "LegalZoom's legal document preparation
service goes beyond self-help **because of the role played by its human employees**" — staff
reviewed data files, contacted customers about errors, formatted output; and (2) the branching
program produced a **completed document** the customer never saw, **for a fee**, leaving
customers "passive bystanders."

*Janson* establishes that a **paid, automated, answer-driven document-preparation service** is
likely UPL in Missouri. It says nothing about a free tool that generates no document.

## 3. Design implications

**Charge nothing. This is the highest-leverage decision available.** Every law-business prong
requires valuable consideration, and the remedy is a multiple of amounts paid. No user fee, no
bundled fee, no per-referral compensation keyed to a user. A free tool has no damages base.
This does more protective work than any disclaimer.

**The intake may ask whatever it wants.** Collecting information is not UPL.

**Generate no completed documents for a specific user.** Blank forms with general instructions
that the user completes are expressly blessed by *Thompson* and *Janson*.

**The output is where the risk lives.** Dangerous: "you qualify," "your company is eligible,"
"you should use an EOT," "this will not trigger [tax/ERISA] consequences," "your structure
complies with…" — *Lucas Subway* condemns exactly this. Safer: state the rule rather than the
ruling ("ESOPs generally require X"); use population framing ("companies with this profile
commonly encounter…"); output *questions to ask an advisor*; state affirmatively that the tool
does not evaluate the user's company.

**Disclaimers help but do not immunize.** LegalZoom carried one and the court quoted it while
denying summary judgment. Texas has a statutory safe harbor —
[Tex. Gov't Code § 81.101(c)](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.81.htm)
exempts written materials, forms and software including "by means of an Internet web site" if
they conspicuously state they are not a substitute for an attorney. **Missouri has no
analogue.**

**Operator identity — and this cuts against us.**
[Mo. R. Prof. Conduct 4-5.5(a)](https://www.courts.mo.gov/courts/ClerkHandbooksP2RulesOnly.nsf/c0c6ffa99df4993f86256ba50057dcb8/84187ab9f9f1995486256ca600521226):
"A lawyer shall not practice law… in violation of the regulation of the legal profession… **or
assist another in doing so**." A supervising Missouri-licensed faculty member acquires
independent disciplinary exposure. **Faculty supervision does not launder UPL; it adds a
second regulated actor.** Rule 13 student practice does not help — it covers appearing for
indigent persons or ABA-clinic clients, not a public advisory website for business owners.

Nonprofit status makes little statutory difference. *[Upsolve, Inc. v.
James](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/10668452/upsolve-inc-v-james/)*, No. 22-1345 (2d
Cir. Sept. 9, 2025) — free and nonprofit did not exempt a program from New York's UPL statutes.

**One concrete step available:** Missouri's Office of Legal Ethics Counsel issues informal
opinions to lawyers about their own conduct under Rule 5.30. A supervising faculty member
could request one about their Rule 4-5.5(a) exposure. Advisory and non-binding.

## 4. What this means for standardized forms

The pivot toward standardizing deal documents sits on **better** ground than an intake
questionnaire, provided two things hold: **the forms are published rather than completed for a
particular user, and nothing is charged.** *Thompson*, *Hargis* and *Janson* all point the
same way on published forms with general instructions. The danger zone is a tool that selects
among forms and fills them in based on someone's answers, for a fee — which is *Janson* on its
facts.

## 5. Genuinely uncertain

1. **Whether a free service can still be UPL.** The statutory consideration element is
   unambiguous, but the courts' definitional power is not statutorily bounded. No Missouri
   case found holding a free service to be UPL, and none holding it cannot be.
2. **Whether conditional automated output is "advising or counseling as to any secular law."**
   No Missouri case addresses non-document-generating automated output.
3. **Whether referral arrangements could supply "valuable consideration."** Not found.
4. **First Amendment.** *Janson* rejected LegalZoom's argument. *Upsolve* held UPL as applied
   to individualized advice is a content-neutral speech regulation, vacated the injunction and
   remanded — merits unresolved. No Eighth Circuit or Missouri decision on point.
5. **How much of this is even "law."** Much of an employee-ownership screen is valuation, tax
   and financing. No Missouri authority located on mixed business-advisory tools.

## 6. *Parsons Technology* — weaker than its reputation

The N.D. Tex. decision (No. 3:97-CV-2859-H, 1999 WL 47235) enjoined *Quicken Family Lawyer*.
The Fifth Circuit **vacated the injunction and judgment and remanded** in light of the new
§ 81.101(c). *[UPL Comm. v. Parsons
Tech.](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/179/179.F3d.956.99-10388.html)*, 179
F.3d 956 (5th Cir. 1999) (per curiam). **A vacated, unpublished district order has no
precedential force**, and the legislature abrogated its premise. It is frequently cited as if
it stands. It does not.

## 7. Where the research is still thin

A survey of published LSC/ABA/court guidance and actual disclaimer language from legal-aid
triage and navigator tools was not completed. That is worth doing. Useful comparators:
[MOCEO](https://moceo.org/) and NCEO's
[ESOP Pre-Feasibility Toolkit](https://www.nceo.org/resource-toolkits/esop-pre-feasibility-toolkit),
which frames itself as not replacing a feasibility study and routes users to practitioners.

The National Center for State Courts frames the distinction as information "explain[ing] the
rules… and your options" versus advice that "tells you what you should do to get the best
outcome" ([NCSC](https://www.ncsc.org/resources-courts/legal-advice-vs-legal-information)) —
useful, but **no Missouri court has adopted it as a test**.
