# Team task list

This is how we know who is doing what. It is the only place work in progress is visible to
everyone else — while you are writing, your changes are private to your copy until you say
your section is ready.

You do not maintain this by hand. Tell Claude what you are picking up and it records it
immediately, before you start, and it keeps checking this file throughout your session — not
just when you sit down. If someone else claims something while you are working, you will
hear about it then rather than a week later.

Ask *"what's everyone working on?"* any time.

**Every item has a "Covers" line.** That is the important part. It says what territory the
task owns, so that work that *sounds* different but *is* the same gets caught before two
people spend a week on it. Read the Covers lines, not the titles.

Status is one of: **Unassigned** · **In progress** · **Finished**

## Unassigned

### Decide what the intake asks
**Covers:** the questions an owner answers, the order they come in, and what each one is
trying to establish. Anything about the shape of the questionnaire itself belongs here —
including which questions get dropped as too invasive or too hard to answer honestly.
*Added by Anthony.*

### Decide what the intake gives back
**Covers:** what an owner sees after finishing the questionnaire. How results are phrased,
what factors get surfaced, how we describe problems without rendering a verdict on their
company, and where we send them next. Anything about output, scoring, or result wording.
*Added by Anthony.*

### Write the ESOP explainer
**Covers:** everything answering "what is an ESOP" and "how does one work" — the trust,
allocation and vesting, annual valuation, the trustee's role, how a leveraged deal gets
financed, section 1042, the S corporation exemption, the repurchase obligation. If you are
writing anything that explains ESOP mechanics to an owner, it belongs here.
*Camron's section. Added by Anthony.*

### Write the ownership trust and cooperative explainer
**Covers:** employee ownership trusts and worker cooperatives — what each is, how each
differs from an ESOP, what they cost, and the situations where one of them is the more
realistic conversation. Anything comparing the three structures lives here, not in the ESOP
explainer.
*Kirby's section. Added by Anthony.*

### Write the local resources page
**Covers:** who an owner in Kansas City actually calls. Missouri Center for Employee
Ownership, KC Employee Ownership Initiative, NCEO, lenders, trustees, advisors. Also
anything about what to expect from a first conversation with one of them.
*Mandy's section. Added by Anthony.*

### Research the unauthorized practice line
**Covers:** how far the intake can go before it becomes legal advice, and what that means for
the wording of every result. Starting points: Janson v. LegalZoom (W.D. Mo.), Mo. Rev. Stat.
484.010 and 484.020, Parsons Technology in Texas, and how court self-help and legal aid
triage tools are structured. Anything about UPL, disclaimers, or advice-versus-information
belongs here.
*Added by Anthony.*

### Decide who operates the site
**Covers:** whether this runs under UMKC Law with faculty supervision, under a partner
organization, or standalone — and what each option means for the unauthorized practice
analysis. Affects what we are allowed to build, so it is upstream of the intake wording.
*Added by Anthony.*

### Ask Prof. Absher which license applies
**Covers:** the license question only. Student IP is assigned to the university for
open-source licensing under the syllabus, so the choice is not ours to make.
*Added by Anthony.*

### Confirm whether the site replaces or supplements the Final Report
**Covers:** what we actually owe the course in December. The syllabus puts 20 points on a
Final Report and 15 on the Final Presentation. Worth asking before we plan around the wrong
assumption.
*Added by Anthony.*

### Check sources, a few at a time
**Covers:** verifying the eleven claims carried over from the project brief. Tracked
individually in `SOURCES.md` — this is not one task but eleven small ones, and more than one
person can work on it at once as long as you each take different claims. Say *"give me a
source to check"* and you will get exactly one.
*Ongoing. Added by Anthony.*
